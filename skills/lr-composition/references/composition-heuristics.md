# Composition heuristics (decision aids)

These are guides for Computer Use decisions, not rigid rules. Prefer the user's
brief and the photo's story over textbook composition.

## Rule of thirds

- Imagine a 3×3 grid on the frame.
- Place primary subject near intersections or along a third line when it feels natural.
- Centered subjects can still win for symmetry, portraits with direct gaze, or product shots.

## Horizon placement

| Scene | Tendency |
| --- | --- |
| Dramatic sky | Horizon lower third |
| Interesting foreground | Horizon upper third |
| Equal interest | Near middle only if balanced; avoid accidental near-middle tilt |
| Seascape with reflection | Near center can work |

Never leave a crooked horizon unless the Dutch angle is intentional.

## Looking room / lead room

- Eyes looking right → leave more space on the right.
- Motion direction (running, cars) → space ahead of motion.
- Tight headroom: avoid ceiling pressure unless environmental drama needs it.

## Edge control

Avoid:

- Mergers (tree growing from a head)
- Bright triangles in corners that pull the eye away
- Cutting joints (knees, wrists, ankles) mid-joint
- Cropping through mouths or chins accidentally

Prefer:

- Clean margin around hero product packaging
- Full hands/tools when the craft is the story
- Consistent headroom across a portrait series (match series if multi-photo later)

## Balance and weight

- Large dark masses feel heavier — offset with space or secondary interest.
- Text overlays (thumbnails, YouTube) need safe margins; when user mentions text, keep critical subject out of lower-third title zones if known.

## When not to recompose

- Documentary integrity requires original framing
- Client/legal full-frame delivery
- Already excellent composition; only straighten
- Crop would remove required branding or QR codes

## Quick decision tree

1. Level? If no → straighten.
2. Aspect forced by brief? If yes → set aspect.
3. Subject intact with better placement? If yes → recompose.
4. Perspective distracting? If yes → mild geometry.
5. Stop when improved; do not hunt perfection.
