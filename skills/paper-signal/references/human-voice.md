# Human voice without invented experience

Write like a thoughtful person making a useful recommendation, not like a brochure and not like a model imitating casual slang.

## Authority ladder

Choose the highest level supported by the user's material:

1. **Owned experience** — the user explicitly says they took, used, visited, or tested it. First person is allowed.
2. **Informed senior guide** — the user supplies data, course materials, and/or review notes but no first-hand attendance. Use close, practical guidance without claiming personal experience.
3. **Editorial guide** — only public facts are available. Keep the tone warm but clearly analytical.

Do not move up the ladder because a first-person sentence sounds more engaging.

## The paragraph engine

For each substantial item, combine three moves in a natural order:

- **Fact:** what is concretely true or currently listed.
- **Friction:** what may surprise, demand effort, or make the choice less obvious.
- **Fit:** who is likely to enjoy it and who should think twice.

Vary the order and sentence length. Do not repeat the same three labels or syntax for every item.

Example in informed-senior-guide voice:

> Machine Learning in Python 热门是有原因的，项目部分确实能学到能带走的东西。不过它也不是“选了就稳”的课：coursework 占比高只代表考核方式更分散，不代表工作量会小。想边做边学、愿意留时间调代码的人会更合适；如果只是想靠热门课名轻松拿分，预期最好放低一点。

This sounds close and useful without claiming attendance.

## Naturalness controls

- Open with the reason for sharing, not a headline restatement.
- Let one sentence carry a mild aside, hesitation, or contrast.
- Use specific nouns and verbs; reduce empty adjectives such as “绝绝子”, “宝藏”, “天花板”, “闭眼冲”.
- Use contractions and mixed Chinese/English only when the audience naturally does.
- Keep punctuation conversational but controlled. One emoji is a choice; six are a template.
- Include a tradeoff. Unqualified praise reads like advertising.
- End with a useful orientation or the next installment, not a compulsory engagement plea.

## Anti-template checks

Rewrite when the caption contains:

- identical paragraph lengths and openings
- one label per sentence (`优点：`, `缺点：`, `适合：`) repeated mechanically
- fake certainty (`一定`, `闭眼选`, `稳拿高分`)
- unsourced collective claims (`大家都说`, `公认`, `很多人都觉得`)
- false personal claims (`我上过`, `亲测`) not present in the source
- generic closers (`希望对大家有帮助`, `记得点赞收藏关注`)

## Three-course caption shape

Use this rhythm for a detailed course-selection post:

1. 40-80 Chinese characters of context: why these three belong together and what decision they help with.
2. One 100-180 character paragraph per course using fact/friction/fit.
3. A 40-100 character closing chooser: which profile maps to which course, plus one caveat about year-to-year changes.
4. Three to six useful hashtags, not a keyword wall.

The numbers are editorial targets, not platform limits. Shorten whenever the content is already clear.

## Honest first-person rewrites

| Unsupported | Truthful alternative |
|---|---|
| 我上完最大的感受是… | 我整理课程结构时最先注意到的是… |
| 这门课亲测不难 | 从目前的考核结构看，它并不是纯考试型课程 |
| 大家都说老师给分好 | 能找到的反馈里，对反馈方式的评价比较集中，但年份和老师可能变 |
| 选它肯定不亏 | 如果你更看重 X 而不是 Y，它会更值得优先看 |

## Caption handoff

Keep cards selective:

- card: name, one takeaway, up to three facts
- caption: why those facts matter, tradeoff, fit, uncertainty, next-post context

The image earns the pause; the caption earns the trust.
